## The problem with seeing a city through a screen

You've done it. You stand in front of something beautiful and old, and you look *down*. You search the name, scroll a Wikipedia article and check the map. You realise your battery is at 31% and your roaming bill is climbing. You look up again and the moment has gone.

We built LORE because the best way to see a new city is with a brilliant local friend at your side, someone who taps your arm and says *"Look up. See those bricked-up windows? There's a story there."*

## Meet LORE

[IMAGE: Pebble, Square and Atlas on Bone background — labelled "Renders. Final CMF may vary."]

LORE is a small device you clip to your bag, strap or lanyard. Put in your own earbuds (any Bluetooth earphones work) and start walking. LORE works out which way you're facing and what's around you, and tells you the stories that matter, in 20 to 40 seconds each. Say **"tell me more"** and it goes deeper. Say nothing and it lets the city speak.

## How it works: Map Vision

LORE has **no camera** on Pebble or Square. Instead it "sees" by combining:

| Sensor / data | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Dual-band GNSS (L1 + L5)** | Location accurate enough to tell which side of a narrow street you're on (L5 helps in "urban canyons") |
| **9-axis IMU + compass** | Which way you're facing and turning |
| **Step & walking-direction detection** | Whether you're walking, stopping or turning back |
| **Map geometry** | Building footprints, street sides, entrances |
| **Places & live data** | Opening times, closures, what's nearby |

Put together, LORE can say **left, right, ahead, behind, look up**. In beta it was correct **[XX]% of the time** across **[N] test walks** in Rome. [Link to full methodology.]

## Designed for the way you actually travel

### Eyes up, phone away
Your phone stays in your pocket or your bag, which is safer in crowds and better for being present. LORE is controlled by **one physical button**:
- **Press:** ask anything ("What's that church?", "Where's a good carbonara nearby that isn't a tourist trap?")
- **Double-press:** "What's around me?"
- **Long-press:** SOS

### Works anywhere (Square & Atlas)
**Built-in global eSIM data in 150+ countries**, so there are no roaming bills and no SIM swaps. Pebble connects through your phone over Bluetooth and Wi-Fi.

### Works offline
Download a **City Pack** and you get maps, routing, stories and on-device AI with no signal: in the metro, in a 600-year-old church with stone walls a metre thick, or abroad with data switched off.

### Battery that lasts the trip
**[1.5 / 2 / 3] days** of typical use [measured on usage profile: 6 h walking/day, 40 stories/day, LTE on 20%]. Your phone keeps its battery for tickets, photos and payments.

### Solo, but never alone
- **SOS:** long-press to alert your chosen contacts with your live location
- **Live location sharing** with people you trust, for as long as you choose
- **"Walk me home":** set a timer; if you don't check in, LORE alerts your contact
- **Satellite SOS** on Atlas (NTN, coverage-dependent)

*LORE is not an emergency service and does not replace calling local emergency numbers (112 in the EU).*

### Quiet when it should be
LORE stays silent while you're crossing a road or talking to someone, and starts again when you're ready.

## Risks and challenges

We'd rather tell you now than surprise you later.

**1. Hardware takes longer than anyone plans.** Many crowdfunded hardware projects ship late. Ours depends on DVT, PVT, certification and the factory, any of which can slip. *What we're doing:* our delivery dates include about 8 weeks of buffer. We already have working EVT units (not a concept), and Pebble ships first because it is the simplest device. If anything moves, you'll hear it from us in the next monthly update, with the reason and the new date.

**2. Certification.** LORE has radios (LTE, GNSS, Bluetooth, Wi-Fi), so it needs FCC (US), CE/RED (EU), UKCA (UK) and carrier approvals. A failed test means a redesign and a retest. *Mitigation:* pre-scan testing during DVT, a certified LTE module (not a custom radio design), and certification budget and time built into the schedule.

**3. Components and supply chain.** Chip shortages or price swings can affect cost and timing. *Mitigation:* key parts are dual-sourced where possible, and we place long-lead orders as soon as the campaign is funded.

**4. Global eSIM data.** Coverage and pricing depend on our connectivity partner's agreements with carriers in each country. "150+ countries" is our launch target, and we will publish the live country list before shipping. Data use beyond your plan is never charged without your consent.

**5. Accuracy is hard in old cities.** GNSS signals bounce off tall buildings in narrow streets. Map Vision is designed for this, but LORE will sometimes be unsure. When it is, it says so ("I think the church is to your left…") instead of guessing confidently.

**6. Fact accuracy.** Our standard is "checked and sourced", but historians disagree and records can be wrong. We show sources, accept corrections and log every change.

**7. Subscription expectations.** LORE works without a subscription, but live AI and global data need LORE Pass after your included months. Prices are set now and you'll be told before any charge. You can cancel any time.

**8. Shipping and customs.** We ship DDP to the EU and UK, and directly to the US, Canada, Australia and [list]. Some countries may not be available at launch because of certification. We'll tell you before you pledge.

If we can't deliver, we will follow Kickstarter's rules for creator obligations: explain what happened, and give refunds where we can.

## FAQ

**Do I need my phone?**
For setup, yes, through the LORE app (iOS and Android). After that, Square and Atlas work on their own with the built-in eSIM. Pebble uses your phone's connection over Bluetooth, but your phone can stay in your bag.

**Which earphones work?**
Any Bluetooth earphones: AirPods, Galaxy Buds, Sony, Bose and so on, using Classic A2DP or LE Audio. Open-ear headphones (like our optional LORE Buds) let you hear traffic and people around you, which we recommend in cities.

**Can I use it without earphones?**
Yes. A small built-in speaker works as a fallback, best at low volume in quiet places.

**What languages?**
At launch: English, Italian, Spanish, French and German [Product to confirm]. More come through software updates.

**Does it work without signal?**
Yes, with a downloaded City Pack: offline maps, routing, stories and on-device AI. Live information (opening hours, events) needs data.

**What happens after my free Explorer months?**
You choose: keep Explorer at $9.99/mo, buy a Trip Pass ($29 for 14 days) only when you travel, or use the free tier. Nothing renews without your consent.

**Is my location private?**
Your location is processed on the device wherever possible. We don't sell location data, ever. Location sharing is off until you turn it on, and it is only shared with the people you choose. [Privacy policy link; Legal to finalise under GDPR.]

**Does LORE record conversations?**
No. LORE listens only after you press the button. Pebble and Square have no camera. Atlas's Glance camera only takes a photo when you press and hold, and shows an LED when it is on.

**Is it safe to listen while walking?**
LORE pauses itself while you cross roads (detected from map geometry and your movement). We recommend open-ear headphones and a low volume in traffic.

**Which cities are available?**
Rome at launch, then Lisbon, Paris, Barcelona and London through stretch goals, then 20 cities in 2028. Anywhere without a City Pack, LORE still gives live answers and directions (with data).
