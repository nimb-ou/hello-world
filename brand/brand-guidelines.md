## 1. Story

Halfway up the Aventine Hill in Rome there is a green door at the Priory of the Knights of Malta. People queue to put an eye to its keyhole. Through it they see a garden avenue, a clipped hedge and, perfectly framed at the end, the dome of St Peter's. The square and priory church were redesigned by Giovanni Battista Piranesi around 1765. Nobody is certain the view was intended, but everyone who looks remembers it. (Sources: [Through Eternity](https://www.througheternity.com/en/blog/rome-travel-guide/hidden-keyhole-knights-of-malta.html), [La Voce di New York](https://lavocedinewyork.com/en/arts/2021/07/03/romes-aventine-hill-showcases-piranesi-and-the-glories-of-the-knights-of-malta).)

That is the feeling LORE sells. Every city is full of keyholes: a date carved over a doorway, a plaque at knee height, a bricked-up window with a reason behind it. Most travellers walk past them, head down, following a blue dot. LORE is a small object that sits on you and says, quietly, in your own earphones: *look up.*

## 2. Mission and vision

- **Mission** (from COMPANY.md): Make every walk in a new city feel like walking with a brilliant local friend who knows its history.
- **Vision (Brand):** A world where travellers look up from their phones and see the city as it really is: layered, specific and true.

## 3. Values

| **Present** | Eyes up, phone down. The product and the brand earn attention only when it adds to the street. |
| **Warm** | We are a friend, not an institution. We are generous with knowledge and never smug. |
| **Crafted** | Paper, metal, glass. Things are made to last and to be repaired, and the details are deliberate. |

## 4. Positioning statement

> **For** curious, design-conscious solo travellers who make two or more international trips a year,
> **LORE is** a pocket-sized AI travel companion
> **that** tells you the true stories of the street you're standing on, through your own earphones, with data in 150+ countries and offline City Packs,
> **unlike** audio-guide apps and phone maps, which keep your eyes on a screen, drain your battery and charge roaming,
> **because** Map Vision knows what's on your left, right and above you, and every story is fact-checked and sourced.

**Category language:** "AI travel companion". Never "audio guide", "tour gadget" or "wearable".

## 5. Brand pillars

1. **True stories, with sources.** This is our proof of quality and our moat (Story Engine).
2. **Eyes up, phone down.** Physical controls, earphones and a battery that lasts the trip.
3. **A friend, never a tour-bus megaphone.** Personal, witty and calm.
4. **Made to last, made of paper and metal.** Premium CMF and plastic-free packaging.
5. **Safe by design, quiet by default.** SOS, Walk Me Home, and silence while you cross the road.

## 7. Naming system

**Rules**
- The master brand is **LORE**, in all caps in the logo and in product names. "Lore" is fine in running text (COMPANY.md), but product names keep the caps: "LORE Square".
- Product names are **LORE + a simple noun** that describes the form: Pebble, Square, Atlas. Never add numbers to the name. Generations get a year: "LORE Square (2028)".
- Features use **plain, verb-led or descriptive English** in Title Case. They should sound like something a friend would say, not technical jargon. They are never trademarked with ™ in running copy (Legal decides on filings).
- Never coin words ("Lorify", "Storyfy"). Never use "AI" in a feature name, because the AI is how it works, not what it does.

| Name | Type | Definition (use verbatim in docs) | Usage notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **LORE Pebble / Square / Atlas** | Hardware | The three devices (see COMPANY.md). | "Pebble" alone is fine after first mention. |
| **Map Vision** | Core tech | How LORE knows what's around you without a camera: GNSS, compass, step detection and map geometry. | Always two words, Title Case. Not "MapVision". On Atlas the camera feature is **Glance**, never "Map Vision camera". |
| **Story Engine** | Core tech | The system that writes 20–40 s stories using only checked, sourced facts. | Internal and press. Consumers hear "true stories with sources". |
| **City Packs** | Content | Offline bundles of maps, routes, stories and on-device AI for one city. | "Rome City Pack". Plural "City Packs". |

### 8.2 Concept selection
Three routes are shown in [`logo/concepts.svg`](logo/concepts.svg): **A, Needle + Wave**, **B, Open Quote** and **C, The Keyhole**. **We chose C.**
- **Story fit:** a keyhole is literally "a small opening onto a story". It also connects to Rome, the launch city, through the Aventine keyhole.
- **Triple read:** it is a doorway, a standing traveller (head and body) and a map pin, which covers what we do (stories), who we serve (walkers) and where it happens (place).
- **Scalability:** it is a single solid silhouette, so it holds at 16 px, works on a 1-colour LED ring and stamps cleanly as a 0.5 mm deboss or a laser mark on titanium.
- **Ownability:** A is generic navigation and audio iconography, and B is crowded (messaging, reviews, media). Keyholes are used in security and real estate, so **Legal must run a figurative-mark search in classes 9/38/39/42 before filing** (see `naming-trademark.md`).
- Scores on the concepts sheet are internal judgement. We will validate them with unaided recall and 16 px legibility tests in the Q1 2027 beta.

### 8.3 Construction
See [`logo/construction.svg`](logo/construction.svg). On a 100-unit grid, the disc is Ø100. The head is Ø26 with its centre at (50, 40). The body flares from 10 to 23 units wide between y 50 and y 77, and the base is flat. The keyhole sits 2 units low so it feels seated, like a door on its sill.
**Wordmark:** drawn from Fraunces (SIL OFL) at opsz 72, wght 430, SOFT 0, WONK 0, then outlined. It is tracked +150/1000 em with optical pair corrections (L–O −45, O–R −5, R–E −20). **Never retype the wordmark.** Always place the SVG.
**Lockup ratios:** horizontal has symbol Ø = 1.5 × cap height and gap = 0.45 × symbol Ø. Stacked has symbol Ø = 2 × cap height and gap = 0.8 × cap height.

### 8.5 Colourways
- **Primary:** Ink wordmark and Terracotta symbol on Bone.
- **Reverse:** Bone wordmark and Terracotta symbol on Ink or Night.
- **On Terracotta:** all Bone (mono).
- **On Olive** (City Pack covers): all Bone.
- **One-colour:** Ink, Bone or Brass foil. Brass is used only as foil or metal, never as printed flat ink for the logo.

## 11. Iconography

- **Grid:** 24 × 24 px with 2 px padding (live area 20 px). There is also a 16 px set for device and status bar.
- **Stroke:** 1.5 px (24 px) or 1.25 px (16 px), with round caps and joins. Corner radius is 1.5 px on rectangles.
- **Geometry:** circles, arcs and straight lines. These are the same geometric parts as the keyhole. Glyph shapes don't use perspective.
- **Fills:** outline by default. Use a filled variant only for an active or selected state.
- **Direction set (Map Vision):** Ahead ↑, Left ←, Right →, Behind ↓ and **Look Up** (an arc above an eye). Look Up is our signature icon.
- **Safety icons** (SOS, Walk Me Home, Share Location) are the only icons allowed in Signal red, and only when they are active.
- **Don't:** use emoji in UI, duotone, gradients, skeuomorphic landmarks (no Colosseum clip-art), or flags as language selectors (use language names).

## 12. Photography art direction

Board tiles 01–04 in `brand-board.png` are **art-direction placeholders, not photography**. Commissioned photography will follow these rules.

1. **Golden hour, eye level.** Shoot with real, low, warm light. Use 35 mm and 50 mm primes and leave natural grain. Avoid HDR.
2. **Look up.** Frame cornices, inscriptions, shrines on corners and bricked-up windows from below. Keep verticals uncorrected when it adds feeling.
3. **Through the frame.** Shoot doorways, arches, colonnades and keyholes. The subject sits behind an opening.
4. **People as walkers, not models.** Show them from behind, in profile or at a distance, with earphones in and phone away. Show a diverse cast of solo travellers, including older travellers. Never show anyone pointing or posed holding the device to camera.
5. **Product.** Shoot on stone, linen, paper or leather in natural light, with soft shadows. The device is a tool in use (clipped to a bag strap, in a hand on a café table). Use no hero lighting beams.
6. **Night and safety.** Shoot blue hour and lit streets that feel calm and inhabited. **Never use fear imagery** (dark alleys, menacing figures).
7. **Grade:** lift the blacks slightly toward Night, keep skin warm, and pull greens toward Olive. Use no teal-orange grade and no vignettes heavier than 10%.
8. **Rights:** use only commissioned or properly licensed images. Get model releases for any recognisable face, and record the location and date in the metadata.

## 13. Illustration style

- **Line maps:** 0.35 pt hairlines in Bone or Ink on solid colour. They are derived from OpenStreetMap data (ODbL credit "© OpenStreetMap contributors" on the item) for City Pack covers and sleeves. The Pebble sleeve pattern in `packaging/` is illustrative until it is generated from real data.
- **Architectural elevations:** single-weight line drawings of façades with **one detail highlighted in Terracotta**, which is the thing we tell you to look up at.
- **Diagrams:** Map Vision explainer style (see board). Top-down footprints with a hatch, a view cone and mono labels.
- **No** cartoon mascots, isometric people, 3D blobs or AI-generated "historical" imagery that could be mistaken for a record.

## 14. Motion principles

1. **Unhurried.** Standard duration is 240 ms. Large transitions are 400 ms. Nothing runs faster than 120 ms.
2. **Easing:** `cubic-bezier(0.2, 0.0, 0.0, 1.0)` ("door swing") for entrances and `cubic-bezier(0.4, 0.0, 1, 1)` for exits.
3. **Reveal through an opening.** The signature transition is a circular/keyhole mask expanding from the symbol (a 600 ms logo reveal). Use it once per session, not on every screen.
4. **Direction is meaning.** Content slides from the side the story is on (left/right). Look Up content rises.
5. **Stillness when walking.** The device UI does not animate when the user is moving faster than 0.5 m/s except for direction cues.
6. **Reduced motion:** respect the OS setting. Replace movement with 150 ms cross-fades.
7. **Logo animation:** the keyhole draws as a disc, the head punches out, then the body drops (D5 · A5 · D6 timing, see §15), and the wordmark fades in at +360 ms. The letters never animate individually.

## 15. Sound identity

LORE lives in your ears, so sound is the most-used brand asset. All sounds are mastered to **−18 LUFS integrated** (earcons −20 LUFS) and peak at −1 dBTP. They are delivered at 48 kHz/24-bit WAV plus LC3 for LE Audio. They are designed to sit under the narrator voice at −6 dB ducking.

### 15.1 Sonic logo, "The Door" (1.2 s)
- **Notes:** **D5 (587.3 Hz) → A5 (880.0 Hz) → D6 (1174.7 Hz)**, a rising perfect fifth and then the octave, onsets at **0 / 180 / 360 ms**.
- **Timbre:** a felted kalimba or celesta attack (warm, wooden) layered with a struck brass singing-bowl partial on the last note. That gives a Brass tail with an 840 ms exponential decay and slight 0.3% detune shimmer.
- **Dynamics:** velocities 70 / 80 / 92 (crescendo). The last note is panned centre, and the first two are ±8% L/R, like a door opening onto a room.
- **Space:** a short stone-room reverb (0.9 s RT60, 12 ms pre-delay) that evokes a Roman courtyard. The reverb is ≤ 15% wet.
- **Uses:** device first boot, the end of the brand film, Kickstarter video and app launch (once per day max).

### 15.2 Earcons (all derived from D–A–D)
| Event | Sound | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| Power on | Full sonic logo | 1.2 s |
| Power off | D6 → A5 → D5 (reverse), softer (vel 60) | 0.9 s |
| Press-to-talk (listening) | Single A5 felt tap | 120 ms |
| Listening end (understood) | A5 → D6 grace pair | 200 ms |
| Story starts | Single D5 with a brass tail, under the voice | 400 ms |
| "There's a story here" (proactive) | Two soft D6 taps, 90 ms apart | 300 ms |
| What's Around Me | D5 → A5 → D6 arpeggio rolled fast (40 ms spacing) | 350 ms |
| Turn cue (left/right) | A5 tap panned fully L or R | 100 ms |
| Arrived | D5 + A5 dyad, then D6 | 500 ms |
| Walk Me Home check-in due | Two A5 taps, repeated every 20 s until acknowledged | 250 ms |
| Check-in OK | D5 → D6 octave leap (Verdigris) | 300 ms |
| Low battery (15%) | A5 → D5 falling fifth, once | 300 ms |
| Error / didn't catch that | D5 muted "thunk" (felt, no tail) | 150 ms |
| **SOS armed** (long-press 3 s) | Rising D5–A5–D6 repeated with a 1 s countdown pulse. **Always plays through the speaker, not earphones.** | 3 s |
| **SOS sent** | One sustained D6 + spoken confirmation | 1 s + voice |

**Rules:** at most one earcon every 2 s, and they never overlap the voice. Quiet Mode silences everything except safety sounds. **Safety sounds must never be mutable by theme or volume below 40%.** Earcons stay at the same pitches in every theme; only the timbre may change (for example "Night" uses a softer felt).

### 15.3 Voice
The narrator Voices are defined in `voice-and-tone.md`. Synthetic voices must be clearly disclosed as AI ("LORE's Historian voice is AI-generated"). We never clone a real person's voice without a written licence.

## 17. Do and don't

**Do**
- Lead with a specific detail ("third-floor windows"), then the story.
- Cite sources in the app ("SOURCES" in mono) and keep Story Engine provenance visible.
- Use lots of Bone, one Terracotta accent and generous whitespace.
- Show people walking with their phones away.
- Use the keyhole as a window that frames something real (a photo, a map, a date).
- Write prices and numbers precisely, and mark estimates as estimates.

**Don't**
- Invent or embellish history, or present legends as fact. Say "legend has it".
- Use urgency marketing ("Only 3 left!!"), exclamation marks in headlines, or "revolutionary".
- Use fear in safety messaging, or make SOS playful.
- Use stock clichés: Colosseum at noon, tourists with selfie sticks, maps with pins and coffee cups.
- Mix more than two palette accents in one layout.
- Use the Terracotta symbol on photographs without a calm area or panel.
- Use plastic, film windows, foam or glossy lamination on anything we ship.
