*LORE Labs Market Research. Prepared 30 Oct 2026.*

**Evidence base used across personas:**
- Solo travel is mainstream: 43% of Skyscanner respondents travelled solo in the last year (https://www.skyscanner.com/tips-and-inspiration/solo-travel-statistics), and the solo share of Hostelworld bookings rose to 63% in 2024 (https://www.lbc.co.uk/business/695e3eaacae74c418a1ed0ac93bc787f).
- Women's safety: 66% of women in one solo-female survey worry about personal safety (https://citynews.com.au/2025/womens-safety-premium-for-solo-travel-peace-of-mind/); 65% research destination safety before going (https://www.costar.com/article/421450354/survey-shows-female-solo-travelers-feel-unsafe).
- GenAI in travel: 29% of US consumers have used AI to plan trips (Adobe; https://business.adobe.com/blog/consumers-embrace-generative-ai-for-trip-planning); 33% of US travellers use GenAI somewhere in the travel process (Phocuswright; https://www.phocuswright.com/Travel-Research/Research-Updates/2025/From-hype-to-habit-78-percent-of-travelers-say-GenAI-improves-trip-planning).
- Unplugging: 72% of US TripIt users want to unplug from phones and social media while travelling (https://www.tripit.com/web/blog/travel-tips/wellness-travel-trends-2025).
- Theft: Greater Rome recorded 33,455 pickpocketing incidents in 2024 (+68% vs 2019), and Barcelona around 239 thefts a day in 2025 (https://www.quotezone.co.uk/travel-insurance/guides/pickpocketing-statistics-in-europe). *Secondary aggregation; treat as indicative.*

## 1. Maya, the Solo Culture Collector (primary)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 31, product designer, London. Earns ~£65k. Takes 3–4 international trips a year, 2 of them solo city breaks (Lisbon, Rome, Kyoto on the list). Owns an iPhone 15, AirPods Pro, a Leica Q2 (second-hand) and a Kindle. Reads Monocle and follows Atlas Obscura and architecture accounts. |
| **Jobs-to-be-done** | *When I'm walking a new city alone, I want to understand what I'm looking at, so the trip feels meaningful and not like ticking off sights.* Also: *find the one great local lunch spot without reading 400 reviews*, and *don't look like a tourist*. |
| **Pains** | Guided tours are too slow and group-paced; Rick Steves is "for my parents"; audio-guide apps make her stare at the phone; Google Maps tells her where, never why; hates "content slop" and invented facts; worries about phone snatchers when holding her phone out on a street corner. |
| **Trip behaviour** | 3–5 nights; walks 15–25k steps a day; books on Booking.com + Airbnb; uses Airalo; plans with Google Maps lists, Instagram saves and, increasingly, ChatGPT; hates queues; buys 1–2 museum tickets per city. |
| **Purchase triggers** | Seeing LORE in a design-press piece (Wallpaper, Monocle, Dezeen) or from a creator she trusts; a friend's demo on a trip; "verified history" as a principle (she distrusts AI); beautiful CMF (Square in Sand aluminium). |
| **Objections** | "It's another gadget to charge." "My phone + AirPods already do this." "Will the stories be good, or Wikipedia read out loud?" "£300 + subscription?" |
| **What wins her** | A 60-second demo walk in which LORE says something genuinely surprising and *correct*, with the source visible; a 2-day battery; and no phone in hand. **Lead SKU: Square.** Message: *"Every street has a story. Look up."* |
| **Channels** | Instagram/TikTok travel-design creators, Kickstarter, design press, Monocle shops, airport concept stores. |

## 2. Arjun, the Data-Savvy Weekend Hopper (primary)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 27, software engineer, Berlin (from Bengaluru). Takes 5–6 short international trips a year (Ryanair weekends: Prague, Budapest, Porto) plus a yearly trip home. Price-sensitive but spends on tech. Android (Pixel). |
| **JTBD** | *When I land on a Friday night, I want to get oriented and find good cheap food fast, so I can maximise a 48-hour trip.* |
| **Pains** | Phone battery dies by 4 pm from Maps + camera; roaming is fine in the EU but expensive outside it; reads Wikipedia on his phone in front of monuments and misses things; too many apps. |
| **Trip behaviour** | Hostel or budget hotel; walks everywhere; free walking tours; uses GenAI for itineraries; buys an eSIM for non-EU trips. |
| **Purchase triggers** | Kickstarter early-bird price ($99–115); a YouTube tech review; a "gadget for travellers" Reddit thread; the open API/SDK story. |
| **Objections** | "Why not an app?" "Subscription fatigue." "Does it work with Android and my Pixel Buds?" |
| **What wins him** | Pebble at $99–149 with the free tier useful forever, the 3-cities-offline model, "save your phone battery," and the Trip Pass instead of a monthly sub. **Lead SKU: Pebble.** |

## 3. Claire, the Safety-First Solo Woman (primary)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Profile** | 38, nurse-practitioner, Chicago. Recently divorced; started solo travel two years ago (Italy, Portugal, Mexico City, Japan next). Joined a large solo-female-travel Facebook group (in such groups, safety is the #1 discussion topic; https://aapnews.aap.com.au/news/women-s-safety-premium-for-solo-travel-peace-of-mind). |
| **JTBD** | *When I walk back to my hotel after dinner in an unfamiliar city, I want someone to know where I am and to be able to call for help discreetly, so I can enjoy evenings out alone.* Also: *learn about the place without joining a group tour.* |
| **Pains** | Pays a "safety premium" (central hotels, taxis instead of walking); fear of looking lost with a phone out; worried about pickpocketing in Rome and Barcelona; her family worries about her. |
| **Trip behaviour** | 7–10 day trips, 2 a year abroad; books small-group day tours; buys travel insurance; shares location with her sister via iPhone. |
| **Purchase triggers** | Recommendation in the solo-female community; "walk me home" check-ins; a discreet long-press SOS; Atlas satellite SOS for hiking days. |
| **Objections** | "Is the SOS monitored or just a text?" "Will it work if my phone is stolen?" (Square/Atlas: yes, it has its own LTE.) "Is my location data private?" |
| **What wins her** | A safety-led demo, privacy commitments in plain English, a phone-independent SOS, and family check-in features. **Lead SKU: Square** (Atlas for adventure travel). Willing to pay for Explorer *year-round* if it includes safety. |

## 7. Cross-persona insights

| Insight | Implication |
|---|---|
| Safety is the *purchase justifier* (parents, women, retirees); stories are the *daily delight*. | Marketing leads with stories; the checkout page and gifting angle lead with safety. |
| The objection "my phone already does this" is universal. | Every channel needs a 15-second "phone vs LORE" demonstration: eyes up, phone in pocket, battery, and it speaks before you ask. |
| Subscription fatigue is strong in the primary personas. | Trip Pass + annual plan + a free tier that is useful forever. |
| Gift purchase is significant for personas 4 and 6. | Q4 gifting campaigns; gift cards; "gift a trip" Trip Pass vouchers. |
| Design/CMF matters most to personas 1 and 5. | Invest in the Square Sand finish and the Atlas titanium; editorial PR. |
