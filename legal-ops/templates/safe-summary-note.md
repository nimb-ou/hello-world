# SAFE Summary Note: LORE Pre-Seed ($1.5M, $12M Post-Money Cap)

> **NOT LEGAL OR INVESTMENT ADVICE.** This note **summarises**. It doesn't reproduce the instrument. Use the **official Y Combinator post-money SAFE** form from **ycombinator.com/documents** without edits to the core form (investors and later counsel expect the standard text). Put any special rights in a **side letter**. Securities law: issue under **Reg D Rule 506(b)** (accredited investors; no general solicitation) and file a **Form D** with the SEC within **15 days** of the first sale, plus state "blue sky" notice filings. A lawyer must review the round documents and the Form D. Non-US investors: check local rules (e.g., Indian residents investing via LRS/ODI/OPI; the EU Prospectus Regulation exemptions).

## 1. Which YC form
YC publishes post-money SAFE variants (sources differ on the exact count; YC's page shows **Valuation Cap, no Discount**; **Discount, no Valuation Cap**; and **MFN, no Valuation Cap, no Discount**, plus an optional **Pro Rata Side Letter**) [check ycombinator.com/documents]. **LORE uses: "Post-Money Valuation Cap, no Discount"**, cap **$12,000,000**. Optional pro rata side letter for lead investors only.

## 2. Key economics
| Item | Term |
|---|---|
| Round size | Up to **$1,500,000** |
| Instrument | YC post-money SAFE (cap, no discount) |
| Post-money valuation cap | **$12,000,000** |
| Implied ownership at conversion | Each investor gets **investment ÷ $12M** of the "Company Capitalization" (post-money basis). The full $1.5M = **12.5%** in aggregate |
| Who's diluted | The post-money cap includes all SAFEs and the (unissued) option pool, so **existing holders (the founder)** absorb dilution from additional SAFEs and pool expansion before the priced round. The seed round's new money then dilutes everyone including the SAFE holders |
| Conversion trigger | Next bona-fide **Equity Financing** (preferred stock) |
| Conversion price | Lower of (a) the cap price (= $12M ÷ Company Capitalization) and (b) the round price (there's no discount, so it's the cap unless the round values the company below $12M post) |
| Liquidity event before conversion | Investor gets the greater of (i) its **purchase amount back** (cash-out) or (ii) the **amount payable on the shares at the cap price** ("conversion amount") |
| Dissolution | Purchase amount returned before common, alongside other SAFEs (junior to creditors) |
| Interest / maturity | **None.** SAFEs aren't debt |
| Voting / board | None until conversion |
| MFN | Not in the cap version. If later SAFEs are issued on better terms, **consider an MFN side letter** for early investors to keep fairness |
| Pro rata (side letter) | Right to buy its pro rata share in the **next** priced round (i.e., the $6M seed). Offer only to investors ≥ $[250k] |
| Information rights (side letter, optional) | Annual financials; quarterly updates |

## 4. Process checklist
- [ ] Board consent authorising the SAFE round (max amount, form, cap, signatories).
- [ ] Investor accreditation verification (506(b): reasonable belief + questionnaire).
- [ ] KYC/AML on investors (bank requirement) and sanctions screening.
- [ ] SAFE signed (DocuSign / Carta / Clerky) and wire received. **The SAFE date is the date of payment.**
- [ ] **Form D within 15 days** of first sale (SEC EDGAR; needs EDGAR access codes. Apply for **Form ID** early, since it can take days).
- [ ] State notice filings (e.g., California §25102.1(d) within 15 days; NY). Fees vary ($0–$600).
- [ ] Record on the cap table (Carta/Pulley) as a convertible.
- [ ] Side letters tracked (pro rata, MFN, info rights).
- [ ] Use of proceeds in line with the operating plan. Investor updates monthly.
- [ ] Tax: SAFE treatment (generally equity-like, not debt). CPA to confirm. QSBS holding period for SAFE holders is uncertain; investors take their own advice.

## 5. Things to avoid
- Editing the YC form's core text.
- Stacking many SAFEs at different caps without modelling dilution.
- Promising board seats or vetoes in side letters at pre-seed.
- Accepting money from non-accredited US persons under 506(b) without proper disclosures (avoid altogether).
- Publicly announcing "we're raising" with terms on social media before the round closes (general solicitation breaks 506(b); 506(c) would require verified accreditation).
